The VPN we would actually use.

October 2026 4 min read Unpaid recommendation

Mullvad is a Swedish VPN. It costs €5 a month, including VAT, and it has cost that since 2009. There is no cheaper rate for paying a year ahead, and it does not renew itself. When the time runs out, it stops. We are not paid by Mullvad, and this page is not an affiliate link.

It does not know your name

An account is a number. Mullvad does not ask for an email address or a password. You can pay by card, or by sending cash. That is the whole point of the product: the company that carries your traffic has as little as possible to attach it to.

The same restraint shows up in what they keep. Mullvad says it does not log traffic, DNS requests, connection times, IP addresses, or bandwidth. In 2023 the Swedish police went to the Gothenburg office with an order for information on a customer. There were no connection logs to hand over. The account number and the time left on it are what the service stores. The path a customer took is not.

A no-logs claim is only as good as the last time someone with a warrant tested it. That visit is the test that matters.

The apps are public, and other people have read them

The client software is open source. Mullvad commissions outside audits of the apps and the infrastructure, and it publishes them. The VPN servers run from memory. A machine that is seized has no disk full of yesterday's sessions to read.

None of that makes a VPN magic. It means the design keeps choosing the boring option: less data, a published client, a price that does not depend on locking you in.

What it will not do

Mullvad is a poor choice if the job is unlocking a streaming catalogue. Other VPNs sell that, and they are better at it. The server list is also smaller than the giant networks, on the order of seven hundred machines in about fifty countries, mostly in Europe and North America. Port forwarding, which some people used to reach a service at home, is no longer offered.

If the job is ordinary privacy on a laptop or a phone, those limits are the right trade. You are paying for a company that does not want a file on you, at a price that stays €5 whether you buy a month or a year.
